# "Why AI Makes Things Up" (TOON3D AI explainer 3, night street + suite) — v1 (2026-11-03)
Facts: OpenAI, "Why language models hallucinate" (Sep 2025): next-word training plus accuracy-only evaluations reward
guessing over abstaining ("I don't know" scores zero, a lucky guess scores), like multiple-choice tests; proposed fix:
penalise confident errors more than uncertainty and give credit for appropriate abstention. Fabricated citations /
books are a well-documented failure. Narrator: Jett, eleven_multilingual_v2, speed 1.1. No captions.

AI will lie straight to your face, and it doesn't even know it's lying.
Ask it about a book that doesn't exist, and you'll get the author, the year, and a full chapter list.
Why? Because of how it got graded.
In training, a confident guess can score points. I don't know scores zero.
So it learned the same trick as every kid in a multiple choice exam. Never, ever leave a blank.
You see, it's like a tour guide in a city he's never been to. He won't stop the tour. He'll point at a random building and make up a great story.
The fix is boring. Reward the AI for saying I'm not sure, and double check anything that matters.
Because the most dangerous answer isn't the wrong one. It's the confident one.
