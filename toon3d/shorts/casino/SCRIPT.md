# "The Man Who Beat the Wheel" (TOON3D style 3: casino interior) — original script v1 (2026-11-03)
Facts (Wikipedia "Joseph Jagger"): Joseph Hobson Jagger (1830-1892), Yorkshire textile man (Bradford); his business failed,
facing bankruptcy with four children. Knowing spinning machinery is never perfectly balanced, he studied the Monte Carlo
roulette tables for a month to find which numbers came up most; travelled with his son Alfred and nephew Oates on borrowed
money; won over 2 million francs (~GBP 80,000 then) over several days (~1880-81). Each table held a 100,000-franc reserve;
when it was exhausted play stopped until more was brought. Wheel makers then introduced movable partitions. He bought
houses in Little Horton, Bradford. (The month of recording by hired clerks is the popular account.)

In 1880, a broke cotton man beat a casino without cheating once.
Joseph Jagger works in the mills of Yorkshire, watching spinning machines all day.
Then his business fails. He's broke, with four kids to feed.
But all those years taught him one thing. Nothing that spins is ever perfectly balanced.
So he borrows money, grabs his son and his nephew, and goes to Monte Carlo.
For a month, they don't bet a single coin. They just watch the roulette wheels, and write down every number.
And one wheel is lazy as hell. Some numbers come up way more than they should.
So Jagger starts betting on that wheel. And he wins. And wins. And wins.
Each table only holds a hundred thousand francs. He empties it, and the game stops until they bring more money.
You see, a roulette wheel is like a shopping cart with one bad wheel. You don't notice it, until you push it a thousand times.
In a few days, he's up over two million francs.
The casino panics, and the wheel makers add parts that move, so no wheel can stay lazy.
But it's too late. Jagger has already gone home.
Everyone knows the house always wins. So Jagger took the money, and bought houses.
