# "The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower" (TOON3D style 4: flat-colour interior room) — original script v1 (2026-11-03)
Facts (Wikipedia "Victor Lustig"): 1925, after a newspaper piece on the tower's disrepair and upkeep costs, Lustig posed as
Deputy Director-General of the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs with forged stationery, met scrap dealers confidentially at
an expensive hotel, said the tower would be sold for scrap in secret; picked Andre Poisson (insecure, wanted to rise in
Paris business); claimed to be a corrupt official and took a bribe plus the sale funds, fled to Austria; Poisson never
reported it. Later in 1925 he tried again, police were informed, he fled to the US. Arrested 1935 for counterfeiting,
sentenced to 15 years on Alcatraz; died 1947.

In 1925, a con man sold the Eiffel Tower. Then he came back and tried to sell it again.
Victor Lustig reads in the paper that the tower is rusting, and costs a fortune to fix.
So he gets fake government letters printed, and calls himself a deputy director of the ministry.
He invites a group of scrap metal dealers to a secret meeting in a fancy Paris hotel.
The government, he whispers, is selling the tower for scrap. But nobody can know.
One dealer, André Poisson, wants it more than anyone. He's new in town, and desperate to be a big shot.
Then Lustig makes his best move. He confesses.
He's a crooked official, his salary is tiny, and could Monsieur maybe help him out?
Poisson relaxes. A bribe? Now that's how real government works.
He pays the bribe, and the money for the tower. Lustig grabs the cash and runs to Austria.
You see, a con is like a magic trick. People don't believe what you show them. They believe what they figure out themselves.
And Poisson? He's so embarrassed, he never even calls the police.
So Lustig comes back to Paris, and tries the exact same thing again. This time someone does call the police, and he escapes to America.
Ten years later, he finally goes to prison. On Alcatraz. The one landmark he couldn't sell his way out of.
